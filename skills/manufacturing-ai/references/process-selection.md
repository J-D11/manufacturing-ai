# Process Selection

Compare geometry, tolerances, material behavior, strength, finish, quantity, cost, schedule, available equipment, post-processing, repeatability, and safety.

- Choose FDM for economical prototypes, jigs, fixtures, enclosures, and moderate-detail functional parts.
- Consider resin for small, high-detail parts where post-processing and material limitations are acceptable.
- Consider CNC for tight tolerances, rigid materials, precise edges, and production-grade mechanical performance.
- Consider laser cutting for thin, flat parts with precise 2D edges; evaluate engraving for surface graphics.
- Consider molding or outsourcing for repeat quantities where tooling cost is justified.
- Consider hybrid builds when printed geometry plus off-the-shelf hardware or cut components is better than a printed-only design.

Do not default to printing. If only FDM is available, disclose that constraint and choose a design that respects it.

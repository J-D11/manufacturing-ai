# Quality Control

Define visual, dimensional, fit, functional, assembly, cosmetic, packaging, and traceability criteria before production. Inspect the actual part, not just a file or completion screen. Record tools, measurements, test conditions, defects, disposition, and rework decision. Final approval requires applicable requirements met, risks resolved or accepted, approved version identified, and files/settings archived.

Define in-process hold points and stop criteria wherever early failure can waste material or damage tooling. For additive work these may include job acceptance, delivered temperatures or settings, first layer, an intermediate stability check, critical material/tool transitions, and explicit completion. Stop for detachment, curling, loose output, blobs, collisions, or alerts.

Remote or camera evidence may confirm machine execution, continued attachment, a visible transition, and completion. It does not prove safe removability, hidden faces, dimensional accuracy, every cosmetic region, bond strength, fit, or repeated function. Complete final inspection after safe cooling, removal, and post-processing, and inspect every produced part.


For each critical characteristic, record nominal value, acceptance limits, measured result, measurement method/conditions, and pass/fail/inconclusive disposition. Link observations to a specific part and batch. Rework requires reinspection of affected characteristics before release. Quarantine rejected or uncertain units so they cannot enter the accepted count; report attempted, accepted, rejected, and pending quantities separately. At scale, agree an inspection plan appropriate to the risk instead of implying that a few samples establish every unmeasured characteristic of every unit.
